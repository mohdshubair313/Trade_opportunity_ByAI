## What's Happening Now
The sector is currently experiencing several significant developments:
*   **Regulatory Enforcement:** The revised Schedule M norms, defining Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP), became strictly enforceable from January 1, 2026, for all drug manufacturers. These norms aim to align Indian manufacturing standards with global benchmarks, emphasizing real-time controls, quality risk management, and data integrity.
*   **Streamlined Approvals:** India's drug regulator has significantly reduced approval timelines. Clinical trial approvals are now granted within 120–135 days, and marketing approvals are completed in less than 150 days, a marked improvement from previous delays. This reform includes removing the need for regulatory approval at the pre-clinical stage.
*   **Harmonized Licensing:** The Drugs Controller General of India (DCGI) has initiated a nationwide directive to standardize drug licensing practices across states, implementing a dossier-based evaluation system to ensure uniform quality, safety, and efficacy assessments.
*   **Strong Earnings (Q3 FY26):** The pharmaceutical sector reported a robust 12.3% year-on-year (YoY) growth in the December 2025 quarter (Q3 FY26), primarily driven by a 12% YoY growth in the domestic market. However, US revenues were slightly muted due to lower gRevlimid sales.
*   **Geopolitical Supply Chain Disruptions:** A Middle East conflict in March 2026 has caused significant logistics shocks, including air freight paralysis and a critical scarcity of container ships from China, India's primary supplier of raw materials. This has led to a 30-50% spike in logistics fees and a cold chain crisis, particularly impacting temperature-sensitive drugs.
*   **GLP-1 Traction:** Eli Lilly's Mounjaro (tirzepatide), a GLP-1 therapy, has emerged as the highest-selling drug domestically in FY26, surpassing long-standing market leaders.

*   **Regulatory Overhang:** Persistent US FDA Form 483 observations and tightening scrutiny from regulators in key markets like the US and EU increase compliance costs and lengthen approval timelines.
*   **Valuation Concerns:** Regulatory scrutiny, competitive pressures, and earnings disappointments in index heavyweights can cap valuation re-ratings for the sector.
*   **Competitive Pressure:** Intense pricing pressure in the US generics market, particularly for key drugs like gRevlimid and Lenalidomide, continues to compress margins.
*   **Supply Chain Vulnerabilities:** Despite PLI schemes, India remains dependent on imported Active Pharmaceutical Ingredients (APIs) and Key Starting Materials (KSMs), especially from China, making the sector vulnerable to geopolitical pressures, trade rivalries, and supply disruptions, as highlighted by the recent Middle East conflict.
*   **Global Trade Uncertainty:** The potential imposition of 100% tariffs on imported pharmaceuticals by the US (under Section 232 investigation) poses a significant risk to Indian pharmaceutical exports to its largest market.
